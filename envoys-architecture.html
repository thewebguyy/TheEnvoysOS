01 —

System Architecture

Critical

The fundamental design principle: EnvoysAI owns the live moment. PulpitOS owns everything that comes before and after it. They do not compete for the same data — they hand off to each other at a precise moment: the end of service. The "Distribute" button is the architectural seam.

// Full Service Lifecycle — Data & Control Flow
Envoys AI
Before + During Service
  • Set sermon series + speaker metadata
  • Load slides and background media assets
  • Configure timer presets for each segment
  • Run live: timers, overlays, OBS integration
  • Record audio/video of full service
  • Capture live engagement signals
The Handoff
End of Service
  • Service recording finalized
  • Metadata package assembled
  • "Distribute This Service" triggered
  • EnvoysAI pushes to PulpitOS API
  • Church gets PulpitOS upgrade prompt
PulpitOS
After Service
  • AI transcription + sermon notes generated
  • Auto-publish to podcast, YouTube, WhatsApp
  • Social captions + blog post created
  • Giving links embedded in all content
  • Analytics: reach, engagement, giving tracked
  • Archive: permanent content history built

Shared Data Architecture

Both products share one Organization Context — a single record that identifies the church, its branding, its members, and its content history. When a church signs up for EnvoysAI, an Organization record is created. When they upgrade to PulpitOS, that same record expands — no re-onboarding, no data re-entry. This is the invisible thread that makes the system feel like one product.

The "Distribute" button is the most important piece of code in this entire system. It is not a feature — it is the product boundary, the upgrade trigger, and the revenue gate, all in one click.

Technical Integration Model

EnvoysAI calls PulpitOS via a lightweight internal API. If the church has a PulpitOS account, distribution is automatic. If they don't, the click surfaces the upgrade flow. This single endpoint is what makes the system feel seamless:

// EnvoysAI post-service API call
POST /api/pulpitos/distribute
{
  "org_id": "church_xyz",
  "recording_url": "https://cdn.envoys.ai/...",
  "sermon": { speaker, series, scripture, duration },
  "assets": [background, slides],
  "service_date": "2026-03-29",
  "pulpitos_token": "if_subscribed_else_null"
}

// Response: if token null → return upgrade_url
// Response: if token valid → begin distribution job

Product Boundary Definition

Critical

This is not optional clarity — it is architectural discipline. Ambiguous boundaries cause duplicated features, confused users, and split engineering attention. These are the permanent, non-negotiable product boundaries.

The single biggest risk in this system is scope creep between products. If EnvoysAI starts doing analytics, or PulpitOS starts doing timers, the architecture collapses into one confusing product. Enforce these boundaries with prejudice.

EnvoysAI owns this — forever
Live service layer
  • E
    Real-time Timer System Countdown, elapsed, target-time timers. Runs during live service. Never moves to PulpitOS.
  • E
    Scene & Overlay Control Slide switching, scripture overlays, lower thirds. The live display layer.
  • E
    OBS / Multi-Display Routing Audience, Stage, Stream view management. Hardware output layer.
  • E
    Live Media Library In-service asset management — backgrounds, logos, slides. Upload and deploy during service.
  • E
    Service Recording Capturing the service audio/video. EnvoysAI records it; PulpitOS distributes it.
  • E
    Collaborative Operator Control Multi-operator sync, staging/preview mode, WebSocket real-time control. Sunday morning workflow.
  • E
    Pre-Service Metadata Entry Speaker name, sermon title, scripture reference, series name. Set before service, handed to PulpitOS.
PulpitOS owns this — forever
Post-service infrastructure layer
  • P
    Content Archive & Library Permanent sermon database with search, tagging, series management. The church's entire content history.
  • P
    Distribution Pipeline Auto-publish to podcast RSS (Apple, Spotify), YouTube, WhatsApp, church website. The multi-channel push engine.
  • P
    AI Content Repurposing Transcription, sermon notes, social captions, blog posts, devotional emails. The Anthropic API layer.
  • P
    Giving Integration Paystack (Nigeria), Stripe (diaspora), recurring giving, giving links in content. The financial layer.
  • P
    Analytics & Intelligence Cross-platform reach tracking, content performance, giving attribution, audience growth. The data layer.
  • P
    Church Website Builder Auto-updated sermon page, event listings, giving widget. Built on PulpitOS content automatically.
  • P
    Email & WhatsApp Automation "This Sunday's message" digest, giving receipts, subscriber management. The communications layer.

The Hook Mechanism

Conversion Engine

The hook is not a pricing page or a feature comparison table. It is a moment of felt pain — the instant the media director experiences what post-service distribution looks like without PulpitOS, and cannot unsee it.

// The Exact Conversion Sequence
STEP 01
Service ends. Recording ready.
EnvoysAI shows a post-service summary card: service duration, segment breakdown, recording file ready. Everything looks clean and professional.
STEP 02
"Distribute This Service" button appears.
Prominent, primary-action button. Unavoidable. Media director clicks it instinctively — it's the natural next action after a service ends.
STEP 03
PulpitOS preview loads.
A preview modal shows exactly what would happen: "This sermon would be published to your podcast, YouTube, WhatsApp (847 subscribers), and your website — with notes and captions generated — in under 5 minutes." All grayed out. The church can see it but can't touch it.
STEP 04
The cost of NOT subscribing is made visible.
"Without PulpitOS, this recording will sit on your server. Your 847 WhatsApp subscribers won't know about it until someone manually sends them a link. The sermon that 400 people just heard will reach 0 more people today." This is the pain point. It's real. It's immediate.
STEP 05
Frictionless upgrade at the exact right moment.
Single click: "Start 14-day free trial — no credit card." After trial: $149/month or $1,490/year (2 months free). The upgrade happens when the pain is highest — Sunday afternoon, still in the momentum of service day.
STEP 06
First distribution is magic.
Within 15 minutes of subscribing: podcast live, YouTube uploaded, WhatsApp sent, website updated, sermon notes in their inbox. The media director has never seen this happen automatically. She tells three other media directors that same afternoon.
The Conversion Trigger — One Sentence
"Your sermon is ready. 400 people heard it live. PulpitOS will make sure 4,000 hear it this week — or you can do it manually."

Secondary Hook Triggers

  • The analytics gap: EnvoysAI dashboard shows "Last sermon: distributed" vs churches without PulpitOS seeing nothing — social proof inside the product.
  • The giving prompt: EnvoysAI shows "Churches using PulpitOS giving received an average of ₦340K last Sunday." Zero-upgrade churches see this every week.
  • The whatsapp preview: EnvoysAI sends a preview WhatsApp to the media director's own phone showing what the automated broadcast would look like. Visceral. Immediate.
  • Peer pressure via network: "4 churches in your district are on PulpitOS. See their growth stats." This becomes available once cross-church data accumulates.

Data & Moat Strategy

Defensibility

Data moats are built through volume and time. You need both. The strategy is to own data no one else is collecting — and make it more valuable as more churches join. Here is what you will own, and why no one else can replicate it.

Data Asset What It Contains Why It's Valuable Moat Strength
Sermon Content Archive Full transcriptions, metadata, series structure, scripture references for thousands of sermons across hundreds of churches Proprietary NLP training corpus for church-specific AI. No competitor has this without replicating your installed base. VERY HIGH
Service Operational Data Real segment timing data — how long do sermons actually run in Lagos vs London? How often do timers overrun? What's the average Nigerian service length by denomination? Benchmarking intelligence that makes EnvoysAI smarter over time. No one else has this data at scale. HIGH
Content-to-Giving Correlation Which sermon topics, series types, and scripture references correlate with highest post-service giving? This is the most commercially sensitive data in church-tech. Tells churches how to structure their content for maximum generosity response. An AI giving advisor that no individual church could build alone. EXTREME
Cross-Platform Audience Graph Aggregate reach data — podcast + YouTube + WhatsApp + website — per church, per region, per denomination Benchmarking: "Your podcast is in the top 20% for Nigerian Pentecostal churches." Helps churches understand their actual digital footprint vs their perception of it. HIGH
Church Branding Library Logos, fonts, colors, design preferences for each church — stored and reused automatically Creates aesthetic lock-in. Switching to a competitor means re-uploading and re-configuring everything. Low effort for you to maintain; high effort for them to leave. MEDIUM — Switching Cost
Subscriber/Audience Lists WhatsApp, email, podcast subscriber lists owned by each church within PulpitOS This is the church's audience. If it lives in PulpitOS, the church does not leave. Switching means losing contact with their own people — unacceptable. EXTREME — Primary Lock-in

The subscriber list is your most powerful lock-in mechanism. Once a church's 2,000 WhatsApp subscribers and 800 email subscribers are managed through PulpitOS — migrating away means manually exporting data and re-importing into a new tool. Most churches will never do this. It is existential switching cost.

How Competitors Lose

  • US competitors (Subsplash, Pushpay): USD-priced, Western-designed, no understanding of the Nigerian church context, Paystack integration, or diaspora giving dynamics. They cannot enter your market without years of re-engineering.
  • Generic tools (Mailchimp, Buzzsprout, YouTube): They don't know what a church is. They can't bundle service control + content + giving + analytics in one product designed for a media director who has 30 minutes post-service.
  • New entrants: To replicate your data moat, they need your installed base. To get your installed base, they need your distribution (EnvoysAI in 200 churches). To get EnvoysAI in 200 churches, they need 2–3 years. You have a 2–3 year head start if you move now.

Risks & Failure Points

Brutal Honesty

These are not theoretical risks. These are the specific ways this strategy fails — ordered by severity. Know them before they happen.

  • 01 Fatal
    Trying to build both products simultaneously as a solo founder
    You will build two half-products instead of one complete one. Both will feel unfinished. Neither will convert churches. The most common early-stage killer for multi-product ambitions. You must complete Phase 1 (EnvoysAI stabilization + Distribute button) before writing a single line of PulpitOS code.
    Mitigation: Strict sequential phases. PulpitOS doesn't start until EnvoysAI has 10+ active churches and the Distribute button is live and tested.
  • 02 Fatal
    Raising EnvoysAI pricing before PulpitOS exists
    If you charge $99/month for EnvoysAI before PulpitOS is live, churches will evaluate it as a standalone production tool — and most will choose ProPresenter or free alternatives. You lose the distribution advantage before it compounds. EnvoysAI must stay cheap or free until PulpitOS is ready to be the real offer.
    Mitigation: EnvoysAI stays at $0–29 until PulpitOS Growth tier is live and generating revenue.
  • 03 High
    Building for the pastor instead of the media director
    The pastor approves the budget. The media director uses the product every Sunday. If your UX is designed for a pastor (who looks at it once a month), it will fail the media director (who uses it under pressure every week). Every design decision must be made for the person operating the system at 9 AM on Sunday, not the person signing the cheque.
    Mitigation: Every feature decision tested with one question: "Does this help the media director on Sunday morning?" If no — cut it.
  • 04 High
    WhatsApp Business API reliability failures at scale
    WhatsApp is the primary distribution channel for Nigerian churches. The WhatsApp Business API has rate limits, number verification requirements, and can be suspended without warning. If distribution fails on Sunday afternoon, you lose trust at the worst possible moment. This is an infrastructure dependency you must manage carefully.
    Mitigation: Build email as a parallel channel from day one. Test WhatsApp at full load before promising it to paying customers. Have a fallback SMS layer for critical notifications.
  • 05 High
    Charging before proving the core value loop
    The PulpitOS value loop is: distribute sermon → audience grows → giving increases → church grows → they tell other churches. If you charge before this loop is proven to work (i.e., before a church can point to real growth from PulpitOS), you'll have high churn. Churches need to feel the product working before they feel okay paying for it monthly.
    Mitigation: Offer a 14-day free trial. Run your first 5 PulpitOS churches free for 3 months to prove the loop. Collect giving data and audience growth stats. Use these as the case study for charging.
  • 06 High
    Anchor church dependency — one flagship church = too much of your identity
    If you land Daystar or Covenant early and build features specifically for their workflow, you create a product that fits one large church and nobody else. The product must be designed for the median church (200–1,000 members, 2–5 person media team, limited technical sophistication) — not for the outlier.
    Mitigation: Use flagship churches for credibility only. Do not build custom features for them. Keep them on the standard product.
  • 07 Medium
    Recording quality failures creating distribution embarrassment
    If a church records a service through PulpitOS and it's published with poor audio quality, they will blame PulpitOS — not their own microphone setup. Distribution amplifies both quality and problems. A bad recording reaching 2,000 people is worse than a bad recording reaching 0.
    Mitigation: Build a pre-publish quality check (audio level analysis, duration validation). Offer best-practice guides for recording setup. Set expectation that PulpitOS distributes; the church is responsible for what it distributes.
  • 08 Medium
    Scope creep between products creating UX confusion
    As PulpitOS grows, there will be pressure to add analytics to EnvoysAI. As EnvoysAI grows, there will be pressure to add giving to the live view. Both will blur the product boundaries and confuse users about which product does what. The clarity of "EnvoysAI = live service, PulpitOS = after service" is a competitive advantage — protect it.
    Mitigation: For every new feature, explicitly state which product it belongs to in the product spec. If it fits both, it belongs in PulpitOS with an EnvoysAI integration point.
